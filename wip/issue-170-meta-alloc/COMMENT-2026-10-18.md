Thanks for the extra detail, and especially for the layer-offload comparison between r11 and r2. That
was the missing piece, and combined with your ROCm 10.1.0 confirmation (same tree we run) we now have
the whole chain. We reproduced it here on gfx1201 under ROCm 10.1.0, so the fault is not gfx1100
specific and it is not dependent on your model file. A short summary of what is really going on, since
it is not what either of us assumed at the start.

**It is not a failed allocation.** The r11 log you posted shows it: r11 offloaded 66 of 66 layers in one
pass, while r12 and later run `--fit`, which sees that 27B Q8_0 at 196608 context does not fit in 2 x
24 GiB, so it reduces `n_gpu_layers` (your log shows 32, 49, 57, 53, 55, 54, ending at "offloading 54
layers"). That mixed assignment is the trigger. The layers left on the CPU have host resident weights,
and our delivery added an op-offload hook to the tensor-split (Meta) device, so the scheduler moves
those layers' ops onto the Meta backend. Your own log says it: `resolve_fused_ops: layer 3 is assigned
to device CPU but derived kq mask flash attention is assigned to device Meta()`. Upstream has no
offload hook on the Meta device, which is exactly why your unpatched build works and r11 works.

Once a CPU layer's ops run on the Meta backend, the split-state calculation walks the tensor sources
and reaches an intermediate tensor that the graph allocator never fills in. `build_attn_mha()` builds
`ggml_view_4d(Qcur, ...)` followed by `ggml_permute(...)`, and a permuted view is a plain alias whose
buffer is inherited from its view source, never assigned from its own `src[0]`. Its buffer pointer
stays null for the whole run, and `ggml_backend_meta_buffer_n_bufs()` asserts on it. So the abort is an
unallocated alias being classified, not a memory shortfall. Your analysis of the "deferred view" was
the right direction, and your patch proved it. Returning "unsplit" for it also explains why your build
then started but still crashed on a request: the tensor is split, so "unsplit" was the wrong answer.

**There were two more bugs behind it, which we found while validating the fix properly:**

1. The Meta buffer wrapper asserted instead of returning a failure when one card's allocation failed,
   so a genuine out of memory condition (KV cache, weights or compute buffer) was a core dump rather
   than an error message. This is the "allocation failures are not caught" part you suspected, and it
   is real, just not what r12 hit first.
2. With `--fit` having reduced the layer count, a CPU layer's ops were claimed by the Meta backend even
   though that layer's *state* (KV cache, and the recurrent state for the hybrid layers in this model)
   lives on the host. The split-state model cannot represent a host input of a split op, so the context
   aborted at the first wide prefill with a GDN state assert. A 5 token completion does not reach it,
   which is why the fix looked complete before we tested a long prompt.

**What we changed (in the next delivery patch release):** resolve an unallocated alias through its view
chain and classify it by the buffer it will inherit, report failed sub-allocations instead of aborting,
and restrict the tensor-split op-offload hook to the host-resident MoE expert op it was added for, so a
dense CPU layer under `-sm tensor` runs on the CPU as it does upstream. Those are two upstream bug
fixes plus one of our own policy fixes. An upstream PR candidate for the two upstream parts is prepared
as well. Note that the fix ships as a new patch release, so nothing changes until you update the image.

**If you want to run today,** either of these avoids the fault, as you already found:

* `--no-op-offload` (recommended; it also matches what the fixed build does on this path)
* `GGML_OP_OFFLOAD_MIN_BATCH=1000000`

`--fit off` and a smaller `--ctx-size` also avoid it, but only because they leave the model fully or
mostly resident, which may not fit on 24 GiB cards.

**How we reproduced it on 32 GiB cards,** in case you want to check the fix on your side too: hold 8 GiB
per card with a small HIP program so the card reports the free memory of a 7900 XTX, then run your
command. The fit then makes the same layer reduction it made on your box and the same assert appears.
With the fix, your minimal command and your full command line (adaptive MTP plus `ngram-mod`,
`--kv-unified`, `--parallel 2`) both load, listen, and answer a 5246 token prompt coherently. The KV,
weight and compute OOM cases now exit with a message instead of a trace, including the partial case
where one card fails after the other succeeded.

**One expectation to set:** after the fix your configuration will work, but it keeps 12 of 66 layers on
the CPU, so decode will be in the single digit tokens per second on a 2 card box (we measure about 7 to
8 t/s at depth 16k for that assignment). The same model fully resident is a different class (about 32
t/s decode and 1780 t/s prefill on 2 cards), so if throughput matters, a smaller quantisation such as
UD-Q4_K_XL or a shorter context will do much more for you than any of this. Your host link should be
better than ours for the partially offloaded prefill, since ours is x4 per card.

We do not have a gfx1100 card here, so we would very much like you to retest with the next release
image and tell us whether it is clean on your hardware. Thanks again for the careful reporting, the
layer-offload comparison in particular is what made this findable.
